## 1. Decision

**Feasible as a bounded extension, not a content-only lab.** Recommend exactly one case: **LAB 011 — The shared exit**, an incorrect priority on the intended Active router, with working initial connectivity. Use a single HSRPv2 IPv4 group, two participating routers on one access LAN, distinct effective priorities and **preemption explicitly enabled on both members**. The higher-priority eligible member serves the virtual gateway after settling. Reject equal-priority and nonpreemptive configurations in this first operational subset rather than silently inventing their history-dependent result.

No failure/restart event system or protocol clock is required for this case. This is not a demonstration of failover time, takeover after an actual failure, or recovery of an incumbent. Source-backed failover and missing-preempt work remain meaningful later objectives. A tiny non-spoiling primer is recommended; a full Academy module is not necessary for this first case.

### Source qualifications and technical cross-check

- C3 3 describes the usual single-gateway client configuration. Do not teach that every OS is incapable of multiple/default-route policies.
- C3 9's transparent takeover wording is about a stable host-facing identity, not guaranteed zero packet loss or instantaneous convergence; C3 20–21 themselves describe detection intervals.
- C3 17's highest-IP summary assumes equal/default priority and initial election. C3 18 explicitly limits takeover of an incumbent. A pure priority/IP sort after every edit is not general HSRP.
- C3 23 and R3 abbreviate the virtual-IP command without the `ip` keyword; C3 24 visibly uses `standby 1 ip 172.16.10.1`. Future examples must use the actual syntax.
- C3 24 shows `standby version 2` on R1 but does not show it in the displayed R2 excerpt. Do not infer matching versions from that omission or reproduce it as a second defect. The proposed pair explicitly uses version 2 on both.
- C3 19 depicts a v1-form virtual MAC (`0000.0c07.ac01`); C3 25 explicitly shows version 2 and `0000.0c9f.f001`. These are different examples/version contexts, not one interchangeable address formula.
- C3 28 GLBP group 10 / 172.16.10.1 and C3 29 group 1 / 192.168.2.100 are different samples. Their configuration/output must not be treated as a single measured session.

Primary technical cross-checks read on 25 September 2026:

- [Cisco IOS XE: Configuring HSRP](https://www.cisco.com/c/en/us/td/docs/routers/ios-xe/network-services/network-services/m_fhp-hsrp-0.html), sections HSRP Operation, HSRP Priority and Preemption, HSRP Version 2 Design and HSRP Addressing: corroborates the incumbent/tie distinction and virtual addressing. Version 2 uses group range 0–4095 and virtual MAC range `0000.0c9f.f000`–`0000.0c9f.ffff`.
- [Cisco: HSRP Preempt and Track](https://www.cisco.com/c/en/us/support/docs/ip/hot-standby-router-protocol-hsrp/13780-6.html), Background Information and the nonpreemptive example: confirms that higher priority without preempt need not take over, and that upstream routing must be configured separately.

These checks validate technical constraints, not course weighting or execution of the proposed topology. The future model and case below are original design inferences.

## 7. Academy, privacy and coverage decision
